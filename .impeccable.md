## Design Context

### Users
Two overlapping audiences:
- **Hiring decision-makers** (recruiters, SDMs, CTOs): evaluating Jo for senior IC or staff roles. They want to feel craft and professionalism without needing to understand the technical implementation.
- **Peer engineers**: senior frontend engineers, design systems practitioners, accessibility specialists. They *will* open DevTools. They appreciate code that shows genuine mastery, not just visual flash.

The right UI move is one that both audiences can feel — even if only one can name it.

### Brand Personality
**Opinionated, grounded, wry.**

- English major who became an engineer — prose matters, clarity matters, "interfaces that get out of the way" is both product philosophy and personal voice
- 15 years of craft with zero apology for the self-taught origin story
- Accessibility as a justice issue, not a compliance checkbox
- Queer, odd, extremely online — the about page has Bill Murray tattoos and Romantic poetry in the same breath
- The hidden console game ("Sprint to Freedom") is the personality signature: elaborate, funny, technically serious, rewards the curious

The tone is dry without being cold. Precise without being sterile. Has opinions and says so.

### Aesthetic Direction
Dark-warm editorial. Not "dark mode portfolio" as a genre — more like a well-designed literary magazine that happens to live in a terminal.

- Background: near-black charcoal (`#121110`) tinted warm, animated grain texture overlay
- Text: warm cream (`#e8e4de`), not white
- Accent: burnt orange (`#ff8c42`) — used sparingly, earns its presence
- Typography: Playfair Display (serif display) + Source Serif 4 (body) + JetBrains Mono (labels, nav, code)
- Brutalist card hover: hard offset shadows, slight rotation — intentional roughness in an otherwise refined layout
- Dot grid background on main pages

**Not this**: cyan-on-dark AI palette, gradient text, glassmorphism cards, sparkline decorations, particle systems, neon glows.

### Design Principles
1. **Invisible craft** — the best technical choices are ones visitors feel but can't name. "This feels fast/smooth/right" is the goal, not "look what I built."
2. **Accessibility as design constraint** — `prefers-reduced-motion` is always respected. Focus states are intentional. Semantic HTML is non-negotiable.
3. **Personality through restraint** — the grain, the hard shadows, the orange accent work because everything else is controlled. Adding more sensation dilutes the signal.
4. **The DevTools audience** — someone will inspect this. The code should be worth reading: clean CSS Modules, semantic markup, no hacks.
5. **Progressive enhancement** — effects degrade gracefully. The experience without them must still be good.
